# Authority Strategy

**Date:** 2026-09-14 · **Status:** assets in progress, outreach not started (site not live)

---

## The premise

This site's differentiator is not coverage. It is that **its facts can be checked**. That same
property is what makes something citable, so authority strategy here is not a separate activity
bolted onto the product — it is the product, made legible to people who might reference it.

The order is fixed and never reversed:

```
Useful Asset → Relevant Audience → Legitimate Promotion → Editorial Discovery → Mentions
```

---

## Three assets that already have no equivalent in this market

These are not planned. They exist, and each was built from primary documents.

### 1. Rechecking, answered for Pakistan

Searches for Pakistani HSSC rechecking return Indian board content almost exclusively. Meanwhile
four Pakistani boards publish detailed statutory rules that nobody has collected.

`/guides/rechecking` carries six boards' published fees, deadlines, application modes and refund
rules — and states the thing every competitor would get wrong: **no board has published an
HSSC-level rechecking fee at all.** Every figure in circulation is a matric figure. The page
attributes each one to the examination it was actually read from.

It also shows one board's **three conflicting official fees** rather than silently picking one.
That is unusual enough to be worth referencing on its own.

**Audience:** students deciding whether to spend money; education journalists; college
counsellors.

### 2. Boards that have no online checker

Four boards have no roll-number result lookup at all. Every competitor shows a roll-number box
for every board, which is an instruction those candidates cannot follow.

`/boards` and the board pages record what each board actually offers, in six capability states,
including "not verified" as distinct from "no".

**Audience:** students at those boards; journalists covering Sindh; anyone comparing boards.

### 3. The percentage answer, correct for Pakistan

The ranking advice for 12th-class percentage is CBSE's `CGPA × 9.5`. Applied to a Pakistani
result card it produces a meaningless number.

**Audience:** students; teachers; anyone linking to a calculator.

## The asset gap worth closing next

Three datasets the site already _holds_ but does not _publish as citable resources_:

| Dataset                    | Why it would be cited                                                    |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| HSSC result date archive   | Nobody archives these; news outlets carry them for a day and lose them   |
| Board result-method matrix | One table answering "which boards support what", with verification state |
| Gazette availability index | Boards publish 2012–2025 archives that rank only on archival phrasing    |

Each needs a stable URL, a methodology note, and a downloadable form. They are derived from data
already collected, so the cost is presentation, not research.

**Blocked until a live result season:** the portal-resilience report and the annual review. Both
require observing a real cycle from a running site.

## Audiences, in priority order

1. **Pakistani education publishers and local news** — topically exact, regionally relevant. A
   smaller Pakistani education outlet is worth more here than a high-metric irrelevant site.
2. **College and school counsellors** — they answer these questions repeatedly and value a
   reference they can hand over.
3. **Education journalists** — during result season there is genuine editorial demand for
   verified board data.
4. **Educators and tutors** — calculators and post-result guidance.

Universities are a distant fifth and only where genuinely relevant to HSSC transition. Emailing
a university because its domain is authoritative is exactly the behaviour this strategy rejects.

## Prospect scoring

Score each 0–5, and subtract spam risk:

```
Topical relevance    0–5
Editorial quality    0–5
Real audience        0–5
Authority / trust    0–5
Spam risk            0–5  (subtracted)
```

Automatic rejection, regardless of score: a guest-post catalogue, visible paid-link pricing,
casino/adult/unrelated outbound links, hacked pages, no discernible audience, or a foreign link
farm.

No decision is made from a single third-party metric.

---

## What this strategy will not do

Stated plainly so it cannot drift:

- No PBNs, link farms, or purchased links
- No comment, forum, profile or directory spam
- No mass or automated outreach; no thousands of AI-written emails
- No systematic link exchanges
- No invented scholarship, award or competition to attract institutional links
- No fabricated studies, surveys or statistics
- No fake press badges, partner logos, or "as seen in" claims
- **No student-level data as link bait** — no roll-number datasets, no name-and-marks
  collections, no searchable personal profiles, for any reason

The last one is not a tactic judgement. Publishing students' results to attract links would
betray the readers this site exists for.

## Sequencing

**Now (pre-launch).** Make the site citable: trust pages, methodology, stable asset URLs,
transparent verification dates. Done or in progress.

**At launch.** Verify every asset URL resolves, is indexable and carries correct canonicals. A
link earned against a URL that later moves is a link lost (§94, §95).

**First result season live.** Editorial demand peaks. Have the board status data, historical
dates and method matrix ready _before_ it starts, not during.

**After a full cycle.** The annual review becomes possible, built from a cycle actually observed.

---

## The measure

Not backlink count. The question is whether a Pakistani education publisher, a counsellor, or a
journalist covering result day would reach for this site as a reference — and, over time,
whether they do it again without being asked.
